3. Rezoning (If Applicable)

Rezoning is when the city changes the local planning rules to legally allow more housing units, taller buildings, or different types of properties to be built on a specific piece of land than what was originally allowed. Think of it as the city giving a property a "regulatory upgrade".

4. Development Options

Option A — Interior Secondary Suite
A self-contained unit within the existing home — for example, a basement or in-law suite — rented for ongoing income while you keep the property. Contemplated as-of-right under the provincial framework and Hamilton's By-law 05-200. On a septic lot, the added unit's demand must fit (or be brought within) the system's rated capacity; this is confirmed in Phase 2. Often the lowest-cost route to a first rental unit because it reuses existing structure.
Option B — Detached Additional Dwelling Unit (rear-yard suite)
A separate, self-contained suite in the rear yard. Rural lots in Freelton are often generous in size, which can suit a detached unit — but the siting is governed by Hamilton's rural setbacks, height and coverage standards, and (critically) by septic capacity and separation distances. A second septic bed or an upgraded system may be required. Feasibility, size, and siting are confirmed in Phase 2.
Option C — The Rural-Servicing Reality
On a serviced urban lot, zoning is usually the deciding factor. In Freelton, servicing is. Because wastewater is handled by private septic, the number of additional units the lot can legally carry is set by the septic system's rated capacity, required separation distances, and — where the lot sits near a municipal well — source-water and hydrogeological requirements. This is not a barrier; it is simply the first thing to confirm, and it is scoped in Phase 2 before any design spend.
