4. Development Options

Option A — Interior Basement Apartment (your goal)
A self-contained basement apartment within your existing home, rented for ongoing income while you keep the property — your stated goal. Permitted as-of-right under Barrie's Zoning By-law 2009-141 and Ontario's Bill 23; no rezoning. The unit must meet the Ontario Building Code (ceiling height, egress, fire separation), carry a building permit, and be registered with the City under the Second Suites Registration By-law 2020-010. One parking space is required for the unit. Development charges are exempt for the first two additional residential units on the property.
Option B — Basement Apartment + Additional ARU — Up to 4 Units (upside)
Barrie permits up to four residential units per lot, so the basement apartment can be paired with one or more additional residential units — for example a detached rear suite — where the site and servicing allow. This maximizes rental income while keeping the property in your hands. If the project is built out to four or more self-contained rental units, the federal GST/HST Purpose-Built Rental Housing rebate may open up — it requires four or more self-contained rental units held 90%+ as long-term rental — explored in Phase 2. Unit sizes and siting are confirmed in Phase 2.
Option C — Registration, Parking & Practical Steps
A legal second suite in Barrie is registered with the City under the Second Suites Registration By-law 2020-010, which triggers Property Standards and Fire Department inspections for Building-Code and fire safety. A building permit is required, and one parking space must be provided for the unit with a minimum 1.2 m-wide access path to it. Confirming the existing home's basement conditions — ceiling height, window egress, and separation — early is the key first step, both for the permit and for financing. These details are finalized in Phase 2.

How you finance the development of your property can significantly impact your overall ROI, as well as your cashflow and stability.

Many property owners destroy their project margins by trying to use expensive personal cash lines or basic retail bank loans.

A successful project requires institutional underwriting before you ever pull a permit. We partner with national commercial lending desks to bypass conventional retail hurdles, allowing our qualified files to access specialized programs, rates, and terms completely unavailable to the general public.

Mortgage RefinanceIf a property has appreciated in value or the mortgage has been significantly paid down, owners can typically access up to 80% of the property's newly appraised value.
Home Equity Line of Credit (HELOC)Is a flexible, revolving line of credit secured against the equity of an existing property. Unlike a refinance that issues a lump sum, a HELOC functions more like a high-limit credit card tied to your real estate.
Construction FinancingAlso known as a progress-draw mortgage, construction financing is a short-term loan specifically designed to fund a new build from the ground up (or fund a massive structural overhaul).
